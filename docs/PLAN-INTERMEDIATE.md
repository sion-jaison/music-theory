# Motif: the intermediate plan (Levels 6–10)

The beginner section (Levels 1–5, [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md)) teaches the parts of music: notes, rhythm, scales, chords, keys, progressions. The intermediate section teaches how to **use** them. Every unit ends with something the learner writes, plays or sings. It also trains the ear far enough to hear a song and know what is going on, and to write music that sounds the way they meant.

It is built on the standard texts of music theory, composition, songwriting and ear training (section 3). Motif teaches their ideas in its own words and exercises; it does not reproduce their text.

---

## 1. What a learner can do after Level 10

- **Compose**: grow a motif into a 16-bar theme (sentence or period), write in any mode, harmonize their own melody, reharmonize someone else's, modulate on purpose, and finish a complete song or piece with an intro, contrasting sections and an ending.
- **Hear**: name scale degrees in context, take down a 4-bar melody and its bass line, recognize 7th chords, modes, secondary dominants, borrowed chords and key changes, and map the form of a song by ear.
- **Write for voices and instruments**: four-part harmony without parallel 5ths, two-voice counterpoint, bass lines that move, parts for transposing instruments within their range, and jazz voicings for comping.
- **Feel and write rhythm**: compound and odd meters, swing, syncopation, pickups and triplets, by ear and on paper.
- **Improvise**: develop a motif in a solo, target chord tones, use guide tones over ii–V–I and modal vamps.
- **Judge their own work**: compare two drafts, say why one is better, and revise.

## 2. What changes from the beginner section

The beginner arc **Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create** stays. Six things change:

| Change | What it means in the app |
|---|---|
| **Every unit ends in a workshop** | Not just one Create stop per level: each unit's last step produces a sketch using that unit's device (a suspension, a secondary dominant, a Lydian melody). The Sketchbook becomes a **Portfolio** with tags for the techniques each piece uses. |
| **Model, imitate, transform, own** | The composition tradition (Schoenberg's model-based teaching, the species method in counterpoint) and songwriting craft agree: study a model, write one like it, change it, then make your own. Units follow that order. |
| **Constraints, not blank pages** | Creative prompts give a limit to push against: "only steps until bar 7", "one borrowed chord", "Dorian, with the ♮6 on a strong beat". Constraints get looser as the levels go up. |
| **Expectation and surprise** | After Huron (*Sweet Anticipation*): music works by setting up predictions and then meeting, delaying or breaking them. Each level adds a new way to surprise (syncopation, a mode's odd note, a deceptive cadence, a borrowed chord, a key change) and the learner practises both building the expectation and breaking it. |
| **Ear training is daily and adaptive** | The Ear spark becomes the **Ear Gym**: functional ear training (scale degrees in a key), dictation and recognition drills whose difficulty adjusts to accuracy, and listening assignments on real songs. |
| **Revision is part of the work** | Projects have a draft, a self-review with a rubric, and a second version. Today's 1% can show "bars written this week" and "rubric score, draft 1 → draft 2". |

Learners also choose a **project flavour** for each level's project: **Song** (pop/rock/singer-songwriter), **Jazz**, **Classical** or **Film & game**. The concepts are the same; the model pieces, backing styles and prompts change.

## 3. The canon: books this section is built on

| Strand | Books | What Motif takes from them |

| Harmony and voice leading | Kostka, Payne & Almén, *Tonal Harmony*; Aldwell, Schachter & Cadwallader, *Harmony and Voice Leading*; Laitz, *The Complete Musician*; Piston, *Harmony*; Clendinning & Marvin, *The Musician's Guide to Theory and Analysis* | Four-part writing rules, figured bass, inversions and 6/4 chords, non-chord tones, secondary functions, modulation, mode mixture, the Neapolitan and augmented sixths; harmony taught together with hearing and analysis |

| Composition and form | Schoenberg, *Fundamentals of Musical Composition*; Caplin, *Classical Form* and *Analyzing Classical Form* | The motive and its development, the sentence and the period, cadence types, binary, ternary, rondo and variation forms |
| Melody and songwriting | Perricone, *Melody in Songwriting*; Pattison, *Writing Better Lyrics*; Kachulis, *The Songwriter's Workshop*; Hooktheory I and II | Contour, rhythm and tension in melody, prosody (setting words so stresses fall on strong beats), song sections and hooks, pop harmony explained through real songs |
| Jazz and modern harmony | Levine, *The Jazz Theory Book* and *The Jazz Piano Book*; Persichetti, *Twentieth-Century Harmony* | Chord–scale relationships, guide tones, shell and rootless voicings, tritone substitution, reharmonization, modes as colour, quartal harmony |
| Rhythm, ear and musicianship | Hindemith, *Elementary Training for Musicians*; Ottman & Rogers, *Music for Sight Singing*; Karpinski, *Aural Skills Acquisition*; Kodály (movable-do solfège); Gordon, *Learning Sequences in Music* (audiation) | Rhythm and coordination drills, sight-singing with movable do, a step-by-step process for dictation, hearing music inwardly before notating it |
| Music and the mind | Huron, *Sweet Anticipation*; Snyder, *Music and Memory* | Expectation and surprise as a composing tool; chunking and phrase length for memorable melodies and for dictation |

| Free reference | *Open Music Theory* (open-access textbook) | A free "go deeper" link for every topic |

Each unit names the books it draws on in a **Go deeper** note, so a learner who wants the full treatment knows where to look.

## 4. The intermediate Daily Set and weekly rhythm

**Daily (10–15 minutes)**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Tune-in | Sing or play a scale-degree pattern over a drone (replaces "match three notes") |
| Review | Due cards from every unlocked unit, beginner ones included |
| New bite | The next unit of the current level |
| **Ear Gym** | Five adaptive rounds from the learner's weakest unlocked ear skill (table in section 6) |
| **8 Bars a Day** | A constrained micro-composition using the latest technique; saved to the Portfolio |
| Today's 1% | One number that improved: dictation accuracy, scale-degree speed, voice-leading errors per exercise, bars written this week |

**Weekly**

- **Listening Map**: a real song chosen for the current topic, referred to by title only. The learner listens on their own service and answers "what to listen for" questions (where is the borrowed chord, where does the key change, what is the form). Motif never plays or reproduces copyrighted recordings, melodies or lyrics.
- **Portfolio pick**: choose the best sketch of the week, give it a title and one sentence on why. It becomes a candidate seed for the next project.

## 5. The five levels, unit by unit

Each level has nine units, a project and a boss. Every unit ends in a **workshop** sketch, adds review cards and feeds the Ear Gym.

### Level 6 · Groove & Line

Goal: rhythm with feel, and melodies people remember.

| Unit | Title | What you do | Ear | Go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Compound time | Feel 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 as beats in threes (a dotted-quarter beat); hear 3/4 against 6/8. *Workshop:* a 2-bar groove in 6/8. Heard in *House of the Rising Sun*, *We Are the Champions*. | Duple, triple or compound? | Hindemith |
| 6.2 | Triplets, swing and shuffle | Triplets in simple time; swung eighths (written even, played long–short); the shuffle. *Workshop:* swing your Level 5 melody and decide which feel suits it. | Straight or swung? | Hindemith, Levine |
| 6.3 | Syncopation, pickups and cut time | Off-beat accents, ties across the beat, the pop "push" that arrives before the downbeat, the anacrusis, 2/2. *Workshop:* rewrite a square melody with three syncopations. | Rhythmic dictation: tap it back, then pick the notation | Hindemith |
| 6.4 | Odd meters | 5/4, 7/4 and 7/8 as groups of 2s and 3s (3+2, 2+2+3); changing meter. Heard in *Take Five* and the *Mission: Impossible* theme (5/4) and *Money* (7/4). *Workshop:* a riff in 5/4 or 7/8. | Count the beats in a bar | Hindemith |
| 6.5 | Scale degrees in context | Functional ear training: hear each degree against a drone and a cadence. Stable 1 3 5; tendency tones 7→1, 4→3, 6→5, 2→1. Sing them; the mic checks. *Workshop:* a melody that leans on tendency tones and resolves them. | Name the degree (starts with 1 3 5, grows to all 7, then chromatic) | Gordon, Kodály, Karpinski |
| 6.6 | Contour and range | Arches, one climax, steps and leaps, filling a leap with steps the other way; the contour line drawn as you play. *Workshop:* three melodies with three contours on one rhythm. | Which contour did you hear? | Perricone, Huron |
| 6.7 | The motive and its tools | Repetition, sequence, inversion, retrograde, augmentation, diminution, fragmentation, changing one interval. The **Variation Lab** applies each to your Level 1 motif so you can hear them; you pick and chain three. | Same motive or a variation? Which tool? | Schoenberg |
| 6.8 | Sentence and period | Sentence: basic idea, its repetition, then a continuation that fragments and speeds up into a cadence. Period: antecedent and consequent. *Workshop:* an 8-bar sentence from your motive. | Sentence or period? Which cadence? | Caplin, Schoenberg |
| 6.9 | Decorating a melody | Non-chord tones: passing, neighbour, appoggiatura, escape tone, anticipation, on strong and weak beats; ornaments (trill, mordent, turn). *Workshop:* decorate a chord-tone skeleton melody. | Chord tone or not? | Kostka & Payne, Laitz |
| 6.P | Project: a 16-bar theme | A sentence answered by a period (or two periods) in 6/8, swung 4/4 or an odd meter, captured to notation from keys or mic, played back over a backing groove. Draft, self-review, version 2. | | |
| 6.B | Boss | Rhythmic dictation in compound and syncopated time; 8 scale degrees in two keys; play three named transformations of a given motive. | | |

### Level 7 · Colours

Goal: modes, seventh chords and extensions as colours you choose on purpose.

| Unit | Title | What you do | Ear | Go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Interval inversion and compound intervals | Inversions add up to 9 (a 3rd turns into a 6th); major ↔ minor, augmented ↔ diminished, perfect stays perfect. 9ths, 10ths, 11ths, 13ths. *Workshop:* octave-displace notes of a melody to make it leap. | Compound intervals | Kostka & Payne |
| 7.2 | The seven modes as colours | From brightest to darkest: Lydian, Ionian, Mixolydian, Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian, Locrian; each step lowers one note. Hear all seven from one tonic over a drone. *Workshop:* one melody in four modes; pick a favourite and say why. | Name the mode over a drone | Persichetti |
| 7.3 | Modal vamps | Each mode's characteristic note and chord: Dorian ♮6 and major IV (*Oye Como Va*, *Scarborough Fair*), Mixolydian ♭7 and ♭VII (*Norwegian Wood*), Lydian ♯4 and major II (*The Simpsons* theme), Phrygian ♭2 and ♭II (flamenco). *Workshop:* a two-chord vamp, then improvise over it with the radar showing the mode's notes. | Which mode is this vamp? | Persichetti, Hooktheory |
| 7.4 | All the seventh chords | Diatonic 7ths in major (Imaj7 ii7 iii7 IVmaj7 V7 vi7 viiø7) and minor; the fully diminished 7th. *Workshop:* turn a triad progression into a 7th-chord progression and hear what changes. | maj7, 7, m7, ø7, °7 | Kostka & Payne, Levine |
| 7.5 | Extensions and colour chords | add9, 6/9, sus2, sus4, 9, 11, 13; which extensions sound good on which chord, and why the 5th can usually be left out. *Workshop:* a "colour pass" over your Level 5 progression. | sus or add9? triad or extended? | Levine |
| 7.6 | Pentatonic and blues mastery | Pentatonics in every key and position, mixing major and minor blues, call and response. *Workshop:* record a 12-bar solo built from one motive. | Major or minor pentatonic? | Levine |
| 7.7 | Chord–scale thinking and guide tones | Which scale fits which chord (Ionian on maj7, Dorian on m7, Mixolydian on 7, Locrian on ø7); guide tones (3rds and 7ths) and how they move by step through ii–V–I. *Workshop:* a guide-tone line, then a melody built on it. | Hear the 3rd and 7th move | Levine |
| 7.8 | Quartal and open sounds | Chords in 4ths, sus voicings, open 5ths; film and ambient colours. *Workshop:* a 16-bar ambient piece on quartal pads. | Stacked 3rds or 4ths? | Persichetti, Levine |
| 7.9 | Ear lab: colours in real music | Listening Maps for modes and 7th chords; short recognition drills. *Workshop:* write a 4-bar answer in the same mode as the Listening Map song. | Mixed recognition | Open Music Theory |
| 7.P | Project: a three-mood cue | 24–32 bars moving through three moods (e.g. wonder in Lydian, tension in Phrygian, home in Ionian) with extended chords over a backing style. Draft, review, version 2. | | |
| 7.B | Boss | Name 6 modes by ear; name 8 seventh-chord qualities by ear; play guide tones through ii–V–I in two keys. | | |

### Level 8 · Voices

Goal: lines that move well together: counterpoint, bass lines, four-part harmony, harmonizing a melody.

| Unit | Title | What you do | Ear | Go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | Two voices: first species | Note against note over a cantus firmus: perfect and imperfect consonances, contrary motion, no parallel 5ths or octaves. The **voice-leading checker** marks problems as you write. *Workshop:* sing your counterpoint while the app plays the cantus; the mic checks your pitches. | Parallel or contrary motion? | Fux, Jeppesen |
| 8.2 | Passing tones and suspensions | Second species (two notes against one) and fourth species: preparation, suspension, resolution (4–3, 7–6, 9–8, 2–3 in the bass). *Workshop:* add a chain of suspensions to a progression. | Hear the suspension resolve | Fux, Aldwell & Schachter |
| 8.3 | Bass lines that move | Root position versus inversions, stepwise bass, pedal points, the line cliché in minor (i – i(maj7) – i7 – i6), walking-bass basics. *Workshop:* the bass line for your Level 5 piece. | Bass-line dictation | Kostka & Payne, Levine |
| 8.4 | Figured bass and 6/4 chords | 6, 6/4, 7, 6/5, 4/3, 4/2; the cadential, passing and pedal 6/4. *Workshop:* a progression whose bass moves only by step. | Which inversion? | Kostka & Payne, Laitz |
| 8.5 | Four-part harmony | Ranges, spacing, doubling (double the root; never the leading tone), keep common tones, move other voices to the nearest note; the leading tone rises, the 7th falls. *Workshop:* a 4-bar progression in four voices, checked live. | Hear the soprano and the bass at once | Aldwell & Schachter, Kostka & Payne |
| 8.6 | Non-chord tones in four voices | Suspensions, retardations, pedals and appoggiaturas inside a texture; read a Bach chorale phrase (public domain) and find them. *Workshop:* re-harmonize the chorale phrase in your own way. | Find the suspension | Laitz, Clendinning & Marvin |
| 8.7 | Voice leading on piano and guitar | Smooth chord changes in close position, keeping common tones; guitar voicings on the top strings; comping patterns. *Workshop:* re-voice a pop loop so no voice jumps more than a 3rd. | Smooth or jumpy? | Levine (*The Jazz Piano Book*) |
| 8.8 | Harmonizing a melody | Find the cadences first, chord tones on strong beats, harmonic rhythm, choosing between I, vi and iii (they share notes), bass-first harmonizing. *Workshop:* harmonize a public-domain folk tune (*Scarborough Fair*, *Greensleeves*, *Ode to Joy*) two ways. | Which harmonization fits better, and why? | Kostka & Payne, Piston |
| 8.9 | Ear lab: harmonic dictation | Take down the bass and soprano of a 4-chord phrase, then the Roman numerals; Karpinski's steps: hear, remember, find do, map degrees, write. | Outer-voice and numeral dictation | Karpinski, Ottman & Rogers |
| 8.P | Project: four voices | A 16-bar four-voice setting of your own melody (choir, string quartet, or piano), or a two-voice invention in the spirit of Bach's Inventions. Must pass the voice-leading checker; review, version 2. | | |
| 8.B | Boss | Find and fix the errors in a faulty four-part progression; bass and numeral dictation; a first-species counterpoint that passes every rule. | | |

### Level 9 · Borrow & Travel

Goal: chromatic harmony that sounds intentional: secondary dominants, borrowed chords, modulation, reharmonization.

| Unit | Title | What you do | Ear | Go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9.1 | Secondary dominants | A dominant for any chord: V/V, V/vi, V/ii, V/IV; tonicization. Heard in *Yesterday*. *Workshop:* add two secondary dominants to a progression and hear the pull. | Spot the out-of-key leading tone | Kostka & Payne, Hooktheory |
| 9.2 | Chains and leading-tone chords | vii°/V; dominant chains around the circle: VI7–II7–V7–I (*Sweet Georgia Brown*), and the ragtime progression, which starts one link earlier on III7. *Workshop:* write a circle-of-fifths sequence and a melody that follows it. | Count the chain | Kostka & Payne, Levine |
| 9.3 | Borrowed chords | Mode mixture: iv, ♭VI, ♭VII, ♭III, ii° in a major key; ♭VI–♭VII–I; the Picardy third. Heard in *Creep* (I–III–IV–iv), *Hey Jude*'s outro (♭VII). *Workshop:* make a happy progression bittersweet with one borrowed chord. | Major chord or borrowed? | Kostka & Payne, Hooktheory |
| 9.4 | Modulation to nearby keys | Pivot chords; the circle shows the closely related keys; tonicization versus a real modulation. *Workshop:* 8 bars that move to the dominant key and come home. | Did it modulate? Where to? | Aldwell & Schachter, Laitz |
| 9.5 | Key changes for effect | Direct modulation (the last-chorus lift: *Love on Top*, *Man in the Mirror*), common-tone modulation, chromatic mediants (film scores' major chords a 3rd apart). *Workshop:* a final chorus that lifts. | How far did the key move? | Kostka & Payne, Persichetti |
| 9.6 | Drama chords | The Neapolitan ♭II6 (bar 3 of the *Moonlight* Sonata), augmented sixths (Italian, French, German) leading to V. *Workshop:* put a Neapolitan before your cadence. | Hear ♭II in a minor key | Kostka & Payne, Aldwell & Schachter |
| 9.7 | Tritone substitution and the backdoor | ♭II7 for V7, ii–V chains, the backdoor ii–V (iv7–♭VII7–I). *Workshop:* reharmonize *Twinkle Twinkle* three ways. | V7 or its tritone sub? | Levine |
| 9.8 | Reharmonization studio | The **Reharm Lab**: the melody stays, the chords change. Substitute relatives (I, vi, iii), add secondary dominants, borrow, tritone-substitute, pedal; the app checks each chord against the melody note. *Workshop:* three reharms of your Level 5 piece; keep the best. | Which reharm is this? | Levine, Hooktheory |
| 9.9 | Ear lab: chromatic harmony in songs | Listening Maps for secondary dominants, borrowed chords and key changes; transcribe a progression from your own audio file in the **Transcribe** tool. | Progression dictation with chromatic chords | Hooktheory, Karpinski |
| 9.P | Project: the travelling song | A verse/chorus (or ABA) piece with at least one secondary dominant, one borrowed chord and one modulation, arranged with a backing style. Review, version 2. | | |
| 9.B | Boss | Name secondary and borrowed chords by ear; spell and play secondary dominants in three keys; modulate through a pivot chord by playing it. | | |

### Level 10 · Write the Song

Goal: put it all together: form, words, arranging, jazz craft, improvisation, revision, and a finished original work.

| Unit | Title | What you do | Ear | Go deeper |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 10.2 | Words and melody (prosody) | Stressed syllables on strong beats, line length matched to phrase length, where the title goes. | 10.6 | Jazz voicings and comping | Shell voicings (root, 3rd, 7th), rootless voicings, comping rhythms. *Workshop:* comp through a ii–V–I tune with the backing band. | Hear the voicing's top note | Levine (*The Jazz Piano Book*) |

## 6. The ear-training spine

The Ear Gym draws from every unlocked skill, weighting the weakest. Difficulty goes up within a skill as accuracy rises (wider ranges, faster tempos, more keys, less context).

| Skill | Starts | Grows into |

## 8. New tools the intermediate section needs

In build order, because each unlocks several units:

1. **Rhythm engine v2**: compound and odd meters, triplets, swing ratio, syncopation scoring, and tap-to-notation (quantizing taps onto the grid). Needed from 6.1.
2. **Melody capture to notation**: play or sing a melody; the app quantizes it to a staff you can edit by tapping notes. The core composing tool from 6.P onward.
3. **Drone and cadence generator**: establishes a key before every scale-degree, mode or dictation question.
4. **Variation Lab and Reharm Lab**: apply motive transformations (6.7) and chord substitutions under a fixed melody (9.8), audition them, keep the ones you like.
5. **Voice-leading and counterpoint checker**: parallels, spacing, ranges, voice crossing, doubling, leading-tone and 7th resolution, species rules; explains each problem in words.
6. **Key finder**: a key-profile algorithm (Krumhansl–Schmuckler) for modulation questions, Listening Map answers, and a better key guess in Toolbox → Listen.
7. **Backing styles**: pop, rock, swing, bossa, 6/8 ballad, odd-meter grooves, built on the Level 5 loop engine, with arrangement roles that can be muted.
8. **Transcribe**: open an audio file from your own device, loop and slow down a passage without changing pitch, and let the chord recognizer suggest chords; the learner confirms by ear and by playing.
9. **Export**: Standard MIDI File export of any sketch (and later MusicXML) so work can continue in a DAW or notation program.
10. **Instrument data**: ranges, transpositions and clefs for the arranging units.
11. **Accounts and sharing** (later): sync across devices, then sharing projects for feedback. Until then everything stays in the browser.

The mic gains: sight-singing with pitch accuracy per note, two-part singing against the app (8.1), and solo analysis (motive reuse and chord-tone landings, 10.7). Chord recognition already covers 7th chords; extended chords (9ths and beyond) are recognized by their core 7th chord.

## 10. Using the sources responsibly

- Concepts, rules and terms are taught in Motif's own words with Motif's own examples. No text, exercises or musical examples are copied from the books.
- In-app audio and notation use public-domain music (Bach chorales and inventions, folk tunes, Classical-era themes) or melodies written for Motif.
- Modern songs appear only as titles in Listening Maps, with what to listen for; their recordings, melodies and lyrics are never reproduced.
- The Transcribe tool works only on audio files the learner opens from their own device; nothing is uploaded.
