# Motif: the beginner plan (Levels 1–5)

Motif teaches music theory by having you **play, sing and make things** rather than read and tick boxes. The microphone (or the on-screen keys, computer keys, or a MIDI keyboard) is the answer sheet: you answer a question by playing the note, the scale, the chord or the progression, and the app hears whether it is right.

This document is the plan for the whole beginner section, Levels 1 to 5. When a learner passes the Level 5 boss, they have covered everything a beginner needs (the checklist in section 7 proves it). The intermediate section (Level 6 onward) is planned only after this one is built and checked.

---

## 1. What a learner can do after Level 5

- Find any note on a keyboard, guitar string or with their voice, and read it on the treble and bass staves.
- Keep a steady beat and read and clap rhythms in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4: whole to sixteenth notes, rests, dots and ties.
- Build any major scale and the three minor scales from any note, and sing them with solfège.
- Name intervals by counting and by ear, using a song for each one.
- Build and recognize major, minor, diminished and augmented triads, their inversions, and the three common seventh chords, and play them so the app recognizes them.
- Name the key from a key signature, and draw the Circle of Fifths from memory.
- Use the circle to find the six main chords of any key, and play common progressions (I–IV–V–I, I–V–vi–IV, the 12-bar blues, ii–V–I) in any key.
- Hear and name cadences, read dynamics, tempo and articulation marks, and write an 8-bar piece (a question-and-answer period) over a chord progression.

---

## 2. How Motif teaches (not the regular way)

| Idea | What it looks like in the app |
|---|---|
| **Play first, name later** | Every unit follows the arc **Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create**. You hear and play a thing before the app gives it a name. |
| **The mic is the answer sheet** | Answers are played or sung. Pitch detection for notes and scales, onset detection for rhythm, chord recognition for harmony. Every task also works with tapped keys, computer keys or MIDI. |
| **A little every day** | The 10-minute **Daily Set**: Tune-in → Review → New bite → Create → Ear spark → **Today's 1%**. The last step shows one number that got better since last time (recall speed, ear accuracy, first-try matches, circle time). |
| **Spaced review** | Finishing a unit adds review cards. Cards return after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; a miss sends a card back to tomorrow. Cards are answered by playing where possible. |
| **One idea grows all year** | The **Sketchbook** carries a single musical idea through the levels: Level 1 motif → Level 2 phrase → Level 3 harmonized phrase → Level 4 minor version → Level 5 8-bar piece. |
| **Memory hooks, not memorization** | Every scale, chord and progression comes with a *recipe* (a number pattern), a *picture* (a shape on the keys or a place on the clock), and an *anchor* (a song you already know). Section 5 lists them. |
| **Boss challenges** | Each level ends with a boss that unlocks the next level. Passing the boss is also the way to test out of a level you already know. |
| **Toolbox** | Always-open references that also listen: Circle of Fifths explorer, scale finder, chord finder and "what chord am I playing?", progression player, and every memory hook you have unlocked. |

## 3. What the microphone does at each level

| Level | New listening skill | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single notes (McLeod pitch method, cents meter), claps (onset detector) | Find notes, octaves, half/whole steps, voice range, steady beat, clap-back rhythms |
| 2 | Note sequences | Read a note on the staff and play it, play scales up and down, sing solfège degrees, play an interval |
| 3 | **Chords** (chroma analysis with harmonic templates) and the bass note | Play a named chord on piano or guitar and have it recognized, including inversions (C/E) |
| 4 | Tonic and scale type | Play the tonic of a key signature, walk the circle in fifths, play the three minor scales |
| 5 | **Progressions**, chord-tone feedback, loudness | Play a progression chord by chord, improvise over a backing loop with live chord-tone feedback, play a crescendo |

Without a mic, chords come from the keys: notes tapped within about 1.5 seconds count as one chord, and a MIDI keyboard's held notes count directly.

## 4. The five levels, unit by unit

Unit IDs match the app. Every unit adds review cards; the boss unlocks the next level.

### Level 1 · Hear It, Find It *(built)*

Goal: hear pitch and pulse, and find any note.

| Unit | Title | You do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What a note is | Make the pitch dot climb and fall with your voice or the keys. Pitch = vibration speed; A = 440 Hz. |
| 1.2 | Octaves | Hear A2, A3, A4; find the same note an octave away. Seven letters repeat. |
| 1.3 | The keyboard map | Twos and threes: find every C and F from the black-key groups, then D, E, G, A, B. |
| 1.4 | Sharps, flats, two names | ♯ up, ♭ down, enharmonic names; name and play black keys. |
| 1.5 | Half and whole steps | Step up and down by half and whole steps; play the 13-note chromatic run. |
| 1.6 | Guitar and voice view | One fret = one half step; find your vocal range and "home zone". |
| 1.7 | Beat, tempo, strong and weak | Keep a steady beat at 80 and 100 BPM with a strong beat 1. |
| 1.8 | Note lengths in 4/4 and 2/4 | Whole, half, quarter, eighth pairs, quarter rest; clap back rhythms (ta, ti-ti). |
| 1.9 | Echo and answer | Echo a black-key call, then answer it (pentatonic: nothing clashes). |
| 1.M | Your first motif | Record and name a 3–6 note black-key motif. |
| 1.B | Boss | Play 8 named notes in 3 s each (7 to pass); keep the beat for two bars. |

### Level 2 · Steps & Scales

Goal: read notes, build the major scale anywhere, sing it, measure intervals, read more rhythms.

| Unit | Title | Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create | Memory hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Notes on the treble staff | See a note on the staff, play it. Lines and spaces, middle C on its ledger line, the G-clef curling round G4. **Staff sprint**: read and play. | Lines **E G B D F** "Every Good Boy Does Fine"; spaces spell **FACE**. |
| 2.2 | Bass clef and the grand staff | The F-clef's dots hug F3; middle C sits between the staves. Staff sprint in bass clef, then both. | Lines **G B D F A** "Good Boys Do Fine Always"; spaces **A C E G** "All Cows Eat Grass". |
| 2.3 | The major scale recipe | Hear C major; play it up. Every step is labelled W or H as you play. Two matching halves (W W H) joined by a W. | Recipe **2-2-1-2-2-2-1**. |
| 2.4 | Build any major scale | Build G (needs F♯), F (needs B♭), D, A, B♭ and E♭ by playing them; the app checks each step. One letter per note, which is why it is B♭ and not A♯. The natural sign ♮. | "Every letter once, in order." |
| 2.5 | Solfège and scale degrees | Sing do-re-mi; sing or play the degree asked; hear ti pull to do. Degree names: tonic, supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant, leading tone. | Do = home. Do-mi-sol is the "home chord". |
| 2.6 | Intervals: count the letters | Count both ends: C up to E is a 3rd. On the staff, line-to-line or space-to-space is odd (3rd, 5th, 7th); line-to-space is even. Play the interval asked above a note. | "Count the letters, include both ends." |
| 2.7 | Interval sizes and song anchors | Measure in half steps: major, minor, perfect, tritone. Hear each with its song, then name intervals by ear. | m2 *Jaws* · M2 *Happy Birthday* · m3 *Greensleeves* · M3 *When the Saints* · P4 *Here Comes the Bride* · TT *The Simpsons* · P5 *Twinkle Twinkle* · m6 *The Entertainer* · M6 *My Bonnie* · m7 *Somewhere* (West Side Story) · M7 an octave minus a half step · P8 *Somewhere Over the Rainbow*. |
| 2.8 | Three beats, dots and ties | 3/4 waltz feel; dotted half, dotted quarter + eighth, ties. Clap back in 3/4 and 4/4. | "A dot adds half again." |
| 2.9 | Eighth rests and sixteenths | Single eighths and eighth rests; four sixteenths (ti-ka-ti-ka); count 1 e & a. Clap back. | ta · ti-ti · ti-ka-ti-ka. |
| 2.P | Grow your motif into a phrase | *Create*: extend a sketch into a two-bar phrase in C major that ends on do. | |
| 2.B | Boss | Read and play 8 notes across both clefs (7 to pass); play a major scale from a given note; name 6 intervals by ear (5 to pass). | |

### Level 3 · Stack It

Goal: build, play and hear chords; know the chords of a key. **Mic chord recognition starts here.**

| Unit | Title | Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create | Memory hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | What a chord is | Play C, E, G together and the app names the chord. A triad is a stack of 3rds: skip a letter, skip a letter. Root, 3rd, 5th. | "Skip a key, skip a key" on white keys. |
| 3.2 | Major and minor triads | Hear C then C minor. Play both; build D, Dm, A, Am, E, Em. Major = 4 + 3 half steps, minor = 3 + 4. | Major is "big then small"; to make it minor, **move the middle** down a half step. |
| 3.3 | Diminished and augmented | 3 + 3 squeezed, 4 + 4 stretched; symbols ° and +. Ear: four qualities. | Squeeze both (dim), stretch both (aug). |
| 3.4 | Chord symbols and shapes | Read C, Cm, C°, C+, then play six named chords. Piano shapes and guitar open-chord boxes (C, G, D, A, E, Am, Em, Dm). | The symbol tells root + recipe. |
| 3.5 | Inversions | Root position, first inversion (3rd in the bass), second inversion (5th in the bass); slash chords C/E, C/G. Play C three ways; the app hears the bass note. | "Same chord, different bottom." |
| 3.6 | The chords of a key | Build a triad on every note of C major using only scale notes: C Dm Em F G Am B°. | **M m m M M m d**. Majors on 1-4-5 ("the Big Three"), minors on 2-3-6, the odd one on 7. |
| 3.7 | Roman numerals | I ii iii IV V vi vii°: uppercase major, lowercase minor. The same numbers in G and F. Play "the V in G". | Numbers travel to every key. |
| 3.8 | Seventh chords | Add one more 3rd: maj7 (dreamy), 7 (bluesy, wants to move), m7 (mellow). V7 in a key. Ear: triad or 7th? | maj7 = 4+3+4, 7 = 4+3+3, m7 = 3+4+3. |
| 3.9 | Broken chords and arpeggios | Play chord tones one at a time: 1-3-5-8, Alberti 1-5-3-5. Arpeggiate F, G and Am. | |
| 3.H | Harmonize your phrase | *Create*: pick I, IV or V for each bar of your Level 2 phrase (the app suggests chords that contain the bar's main note); play it back with chords. | |
| 3.B | Boss | Play 6 named triads (5 to pass); name 8 chord qualities by ear (6 to pass); play 4 Roman numerals in G or F (3 to pass). | |

### Level 4 · The Clock

Goal: key signatures, the Circle of Fifths, and the three minors.

| Unit | Title | Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create | Memory hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Key signatures | Why write F♯ once instead of every time; sharps and flats always appear in the same order. | Sharps **F C G D A E B** "Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle"; flats are the same backwards: "Battle Ends And Down Goes Charles' Father". |
| 4.2 | Read the key | See a signature, play its tonic. | Sharp keys: **last sharp, then up a half step**. Flat keys: **second-to-last flat**. F has one flat; C has none. |
| 4.3 | The Circle of Fifths | The 12 major keys on a clock. Clockwise = up a 5th and one more sharp; anticlockwise = one more flat. Walk the circle: play C, G, D, A, E, B. | **C G D A E B**: "Cats Go Down Alleys Eating Birds". Flat side: **F**, then **B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭** (spells BEAD-G). |
| 4.4 | Clock maths and the far side | The hour is the number of sharps (G at 1 o'clock has 1♯). On the flat side, 12 minus the hour is the number of flats. Enharmonic keys at the bottom (B/C♭, F♯/G♭, D♭/C♯). **Fill the clock** game, timed against your best. | "The time tells the signature." |
| 4.5 | Neighbours are family | A key and its two neighbours are I, IV and V; the minors under them are vi, ii and iii. Use the circle to find and play the six chords of any key. | The **family slice**: three outside, three inside. |
| 4.6 | Natural minor and relative keys | Start a major scale on its 6th note (la): A minor from C major. Shares the key signature; sits on the inner ring. Play A minor, then E minor. | Recipe **2-1-2-2-1-2-2**. Relative minor = **down 3 half steps**. |
| 4.7 | Three minors | Natural, harmonic (raised 7th: a leading tone and an exotic gap), melodic (raise 6 and 7 going up, natural coming down). Play A harmonic minor; ear: which minor? | Harmonic = natural with **♯7**; melodic up = natural with **♯6 ♯7**. |
| 4.8 | Parallel keys and mood | C major and C minor share a tonic. Minor = major with ♭3, ♭6, ♭7. Hear a tune flip from major to minor. | "Lower 3, 6 and 7." |
| 4.9 | Chords in minor keys | i ii° III iv v VI VII; borrow a major V from harmonic minor for a stronger pull. Play i–iv–V–i in A minor (Am Dm E Am). | Same family slice, read from the inner ring. |
| 4.C | Minor mood | *Create*: turn your phrase into its parallel minor; keep the changes you like; save as a new sketch. | |
| 4.B | Boss | Fill the clock (all 12 in under 90 s); name 8 key signatures (7 to pass); play the tonic for 4 signatures (3 to pass). | |

### Level 5 · Make It Move

Goal: progressions, cadences, phrases and form, ending with your own 8-bar piece.

| Unit | Title | Hear → Echo → Explore → Name → Create | Memory hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Home, away, tension | Tonic (home), subdominant (away), dominant (tension). Play I–IV–V–I in C chord by chord; the app follows. | **T → S → D → T**. |
| 5.2 | The four-chord loop | I–V–vi–IV and its rotations vi–IV–I–V and I–vi–IV–V. Play it in G; hear songs built on it. | *Let It Be*, *Don't Stop Believin'*, *Someone Like You*; *Stand By Me* (I–vi–IV–V); *Zombie* (vi–IV–I–V). |
| 5.3 | Twelve-bar blues | I I I I · IV IV I I · V IV I V with 7th chords. Improvise with the blues scale over the backing. | Blues scale **1 ♭3 4 ♭5 5 ♭7**. |
| 5.4 | ii–V–I and the Canon | The jazz cadence Dm7–G7–Cmaj7; Pachelbel's I–V–vi–iii–IV–I–IV–V. | |
| 5.5 | Cadences | Authentic V–I (full stop), plagal IV–I ("Amen"), half ending on V (comma, question), deceptive V–vi (surprise). Ear: which cadence? | Punctuation marks. |
| 5.6 | Transpose anything | Numbers plus the circle move any progression to any key. Play I–IV–V–I in D and F. Guitar capo note. | "Keep the numbers, change the key." |
| 5.7 | Melody meets chords | Chord tones on strong beats, passing notes between. Improvise over a looping I–V–vi–IV with **chord-tone radar**: each note you play lights green (in the chord), blue (in the key) or red (outside). | |
| 5.8 | Dynamics, tempo and articulation | p, mp, mf, f, ff, crescendo, diminuendo; staccato, legato, accent; Largo, Andante, Moderato, Allegro, Presto. Mic task: play a crescendo, soft to loud. | "Piano = quiet, forte = strong." |
| 5.9 | Phrases, periods and form | Question (ends on V, a half cadence) + answer (ends on I, authentic) = a period. Repetition and contrast; verse/chorus, AABA; repeat signs, first and second endings. | |
| 5.P | Your 8-bar piece | *Capstone*: pick a key and a progression, grow your motif into a question (bars 1–4, ending on V) and an answer (bars 5–8, ending on I) over a backing loop; save and play back with chords. | |
| 5.B | Final boss | Play I–IV–V–I in a random key; name 6 cadences by ear (5 to pass); turn numbers into chords in 3 keys using the circle (5 of 6). Passing marks the beginner section complete. | |

## 5. Remember any scale, chord or progression

Three kinds of hook for everything, collected in the Toolbox as you unlock them:

**Recipes (count half steps)**

| Thing | Recipe | Degrees |
|---|---|---|
| Major scale | 2-2-1-2-2-2-1 | 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 |
| Natural minor | 2-1-2-2-1-2-2 | 1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7 |
| Harmonic minor | 2-1-2-2-1-3-1 | 1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 7 |
| Melodic minor (up) | 2-1-2-2-2-2-1 | 1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 7 |
| Major pentatonic | 2-2-3-2-3 | 1 2 3 5 6 (the black keys from G♭) |
| Minor pentatonic | 3-2-2-3-2 | 1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7 |
| Blues | 3-2-1-1-3-2 | 1 ♭3 4 ♭5 5 ♭7 |
| Major triad | 4+3 | 1 3 5 |
| Minor triad | 3+4 | 1 ♭3 5 |
| Diminished | 3+3 | 1 ♭3 ♭5 |
| Augmented | 4+4 | 1 3 ♯5 |
| Major 7th | 4+3+4 | 1 3 5 7 |
| Dominant 7th | 4+3+3 | 1 3 5 ♭7 |
| Minor 7th | 3+4+3 | 1 ♭3 5 ♭7 |

**Pictures**: scale and chord shapes light up on the dock keyboard; the Circle of Fifths is a clock you can tap; a key's six chords are one slice of it.

**Anchors and sayings**: the interval songs (2.7), staff sayings (2.1, 2.2), order of sharps and flats (4.1), the key-naming tricks (4.2), "Cats Go Down Alleys Eating Birds" (4.3), M m m M M m d (3.6), and songs for each progression (5.2).

**Practice that makes them stick**: you build every scale and chord yourself before the app shows the answer (generation), cards come back on a spaced schedule (retrieval), and the Daily Set mixes old and new topics (interleaving).

## 8. Definition of done for the beginner section

- Every unit in this plan exists in the app, can be completed with the mic **or** with keys only, and adds review cards.
- Each level's boss unlocks the next level; passing the Level 5 boss marks the beginner section complete.
- Chord recognition passes its synthetic test suite (all 24 major and minor triads, other qualities, inversions) and is checked by hand on a real piano and guitar.
- The Toolbox covers the Circle of Fifths, scales, chords, progressions and memory hooks.
- Automated tests walk every unit of every level and finish with no runtime errors.

When this is done and checked, the next step is to plan the intermediate section.
